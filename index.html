Year in review: 2025-26 in photos

Look back at the last academic year through the lens of Spectator’s photojournalists.

By Audrea Chen and Pedro Meerbaum

August 30, 2026

Dusk settling over campus. Zohar Ford / Columbia Daily Spectator.

Over the course of the last academic year, Spectator’s photojournalists captured Columbia’s campus as the University redefined itself amid tension and federal scrutiny.

A fifth president in four years. Faculty lined outside of the campus gates, closed to the public for the third consecutive year. Students rallying in support of academic freedom and against Immigration and Customs Enforcement on campus.

Spectator invites you to reflect on the 2025-26 academic year with some of the strongest visual pieces from its photojournalists.

Columbia’s first-ever performative male contest, hosted by Hassan Idris, CC ’29, brought together a sea of students with tote bags and matcha whisks at the ready.

Photo by Esmeralda Paredes.

Raising Cane’s entered Columbia’s local food scene, eliciting excitement from students and community members alike.

Photo by Esmeralda Paredes.

Manhattan’s Community Board 9 oversees Morningside Heights, Manhattanville, and Hamilton Heights. CB9 has grown increasingly critical of Columbia’s closed gates.

Photo by Tianqi Wang.

The Institute for the Study of Sexuality and Gender hosted its first-ever drag ball in the Italian Academy’s Teatro.

Photo by Esmeralda Paredes.

Faculty members held a “silent vigil” for Mohsen Mahdawi, GS ’25, SIPA ’27, and Mahmoud Khalil, SIPA ’24, outside the Broadway and 116th Street gates ahead of Mahdawi’s October immigration-related court hearing.

Photo by Stella Ragas.

Football brought home a win against Georgetown University during its first home game of the season.

Photo by Giada Robinson.

Student Workers of Columbia-United Auto Workers held a picket on campus while negotiations with the University persisted. Rules Administrator Gregory Wawro sent out 20 disciplinary letters in late November following the picket.

Photo by Stella Ragas.

Homecoming 2025 brought a sea of celebrating students and a 35-21 defeat to the University of Pennsylvania’s Quakers.

Photo by Giada Robinson.

Ijoya, Columbia’s African dance team, hosted its second annual showcase, “X: Legacy.” The showcase featured performances from Columbia’s hip-hop group Raw Elementz and the University of Pennsylvania’s African dance team, West African Vibe.

Photo by Audrea Chen.

Columbia students gathered for the annual Tree Lighting ceremony. Outside of the gates, students protested the exclusion of the Morningside Heights community from the ceremony as Columbia’s gates remained closed for the 2025-2026 academic year.

Photo by Marcus Bui.

Students returned from winter break to a snowy campus, with the first Monday of the spring semester having been moved online.

Photo by Jackson Dilks.

Broadway and 114th Street, covered in snow.

Photo by Tianqi Wang.

Women’s swim and dive faced off against Cornell but were quashed in a 197-103 defeat. The Lions went on to place seventh in the Ivy League championship.

Photo by Audrea Chen.

Nurses gathered outside of Mount Sinai Morningside hospital in support of a citywide strike over benefit cuts. The strike lasted one month before a contract was signed.

Photo by Audrea Chen.

Jennifer Mnookin, former chancellor of the University of Wisconsin-Madison, was unanimously selected by the board of trustees as Columbia’s next permanent University president. She is the University’s fifth president in four years.

Photo by Audrea Chen.

Students, faculty members, and community members came together to protest ICE outside of Columbia’s gates amid a surge in nationwide operations.

Photo by Zohar Ford.

Survivors of former Columbia OB-GYN Robert Hadden’s abuse gathered outside of the Earl Hall gates to protest the 156-page independent report into the doctor’s abuse, released by the University in March, two and a half years after it was commissioned.

Photo by Audrea Chen.

New York Police Department officers arrested 12 individuals, including faculty members and students, who blocked the road during an “ICE off campus” rally.

Photo by Eliza Lamster.

A model walks down the runway during Fig Magazine’s inaugural fashion show, “Memory of Light.” Student models donned clothes designed by students from Columbia, Parsons School of Design, and the Fashion Institute of Technology.

Photo by Giada Robinson.

Men’s wrestling hosted the 2026 Ivy League tournament and placed second. The Light Blue had an undefeated season, and head coach Donny Pritzlaff was named NCAA Division I men’s national coach of the year.

Photo by Jackson Dilks.

SWC-UAW organized a protest around the Sundial, demanding stronger protections for international students and calling for the University to declare itself a sanctuary campus.

Photo by Jackson Dilks.

Michael Zheng, CC ’26, in a game against Pepperdine University. Men’s tennis ended its season 6-1, winning its third consecutive Ivy League title. Zheng, Columbia’s breakout tennis star, made it to the third round of the Wimbledon Championships.

Photo by Jackson Dilks.

Fliss Henderson dominates in a game against Brown University, where women’s basketball won 68-52. The Lions fell to Harvard in the Ivy Madness semifinal but won the Women’s Basketball Invitational Tournament. The 2025-26 season was the first since 2023 that the team did not make it to the Ivy Madness final.

Photo by Jackson Dilks.

Bacchanal 2026 was headlined by rapper Waka Flocka Flame, after a last-minute cancellation the night before by English rapper Fake Mink.

Photo by Logan Fencer.

Dressed to the nines, Columbia students flocked to fraternity townhouses to celebrate Bacchanal. Spectator documented student style throughout the day.

Photo by London Connolly.

Students enjoy the sunny lawns during Bacchanal 2026.

Photo by Salem Maru.

The Riverside Park goats made a special appearance at the Riverside Park Conservatory’s launch celebration of its 7.5-acre forest restoration project along Riverside Drive.

Photo by Sofia Wadler.

Pro-Palestinian demonstrators interrupted a panel hosted by Columbia’s chapter of Students Supporting Israel that featured two members of the Israeli military. Two demonstrators—one who directly interrupted the event and another who held a banner outside the event in Lerner Hall—were escorted out the building, joining a 30-person demonstration outside of Lerner.

Photo by Tianqi Wang.

Students protested the use of an AI voice to read out graduates’ names across the University’s graduation ceremonies.

Photo by Zohar Ford.

Spring, on campus.

Photo by Ming Yuan.
